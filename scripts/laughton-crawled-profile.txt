He successfully transitioned his operationally-minded REO team into a traditional sales model by 2013, focusing heavily on lead generation and CRM implementation. Today, The Laughton Team has 200 agents primarily in Arizona, averaging about $900 million in annual volume and around 2,000 transactions. A core principle of Laughton&#8217;s leadership is the constant anticipation of market shifts, employing a strategy of firing &#8220;bullets than cannonballs&#8221; to test and refine business pillars. His mindset is one of abundance, famously viewing the iBuyer phenomenon in Phoenix (its epicenter) as an opportunity. His strategy was to &#8220;make friends with them&#8221; (Opendoor, Zillow Offers) and position his team to capture the high volume of seller leads generated from their marketing. The team&#8217;s structure is built on interdependence, rejecting the traditional independent contractor model. Laughton seeks agents with a &#8220;strong desire to win&#8221; (economic competitiveness). His management philosophy involves scaling into multiple satellite &#8220;pods&#8221; with a sales leader-to-agent ratio in the 30-40 range, which he considers the breaking point for quality connection and retention. He shares proprietary systems for the benefit of the listeners, notably the Reverse Offer Playbook. This strategy is used to combat canceled and aged listings by generating a seller-initiated offer to prospective buyers and their agents who have shown interest. His final advice centers on the non-negotiable need for consistent investment in coaching and development. How Is Zillow Deciding Which Premier Agents Get Insant Offers Leads? | Inman Real Estate News Inman On Tour Nashville is right around the corner! INTEL: Reffkin&#8217;s national MLS idea Clear Collaboration: Haber op-ed Oppenheim on Compass BURGESS: 5 ways to show up Agent How is Zillow deciding which Premier Agents get Instant Offers leads? Becky Garcia of The Garcia Group and George Laughton of the Laughton Team adapted early to Zillow&#039;s Instant Offers program. Here&#039;s how they reaped the benefits Ash Ponders for Inman by Patrick Kearns June 15, 2018 SHARE Share this story via email From: To: You successfully shared the article. Close Becky Garcia, an Arizona-based agent with The Garcia Group, and George Laughton, of the Laughton Team, became early adapters of Zillow&#8217;s Instant Offers program. Here&#8217;s how shrewd business sense and steely focus helped them reap the benefits. Inman Events Inman On Tour Nashville delivers insights and strategies for agents & leaders navigating today’s market. Zillow is using its Premier Agent program to reward certain real estate agent customers with new leads from its expanding Instant Offers effort, but it’s not clear behind the scenes what criteria the company is using to select the recipients. Becky Garcia, the Arizona-based agent with The Garcia Group, has been one of the early agents to reap the benefits of Zillow’s Instant Offers . She said she got there because she’s always been ready and willing to adapt her business. When she was doing traditional sales, she saw the rise of short sales on the horizon and then jumped on the wave of real-estate owned (REO) properties that followed. “Back when I was heavy doing REO listings, you could kind of see the writing on the wall that that wasn’t going to be around much longer,” Garcia said. “You just have to stay ahead and adjust accordingly.” Then one day she happened to be at an REO event, and Zillow was there, touting all of the benefits of advertising with the company. “At the time, one of my colleagues was at the event with me, and she was like, ‘You need to do Zillow!’” she recalled. So Garcia began investing in advertising with Zillow Premier Agent, a paid program that gives select agents top placement on Zillow listings. After learning about Zillow’s new Instant Offers program, she reached out to her local Zillow rep, and now she’s receiving leads from sellers who are coming in through that door. When a seller decides not to accept the Seattle tech giant’s instant cash offer, Garcia gets an email and information about the home. She’s gotten over 90 leads and already converted two. She expects more are on their way. “We have a few others that are in the process, they say their going to list with us,” she said. George Laughton from The Laughton Team has also invested in his business through the Premier Agent advertising program and thinks it’s given his company the biggest return on investment. “The Premier Agent piece of it is our best return on investment, when we look at it compared to pay-per-click, Facebook and other social media,” he said. “The return on investment is above and beyond those two.” Laughton’s faith in Zillow as a tool for his business was paid back when the company selected him to list Zillow’s first few properties in the Phoenix area. However, Laughton did not specify to Inman why he was was chosen, other than that he was a Premier Agent. “We couldn’t be happier about the return our partner brokers and agents are getting as result of just a couple of months of participation in these early tests of our Instant Offers program,” a spokesperson from Zillow told Inman. “As we expand, we’re working on ways to make sure more agents and brokers are involved.” In Las Vegas, the second market for Instant Offers, Zillow announced it will partner with Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices Nevada Properties and Coldwell Banker Premier Realty (Las Vegas) to generate leads for agents using Instant Offers. Zillow’s use of the Premier Agent program in this new way — as a dangling carrot for a select few chosen agents — comes at a time when the program and ad practices generally are under scrutiny from government officials. The New York Department of State was reportedly looking at online ad practices of Zillow subsidiary StreetEasy, in addition to the industry in general, and the subject also came up at the real estate competition workshop in Washington, D.C. , namely from Zillow rival Redfin. “For us, we view the portals as a tax we have to pay,” Redfin CEO Glenn Kelman said, of the costs of advertising on websites like Zillow and realtor.com. “Every dollar we have to pay the portals is a dollar we have to charge the consumer.” Email Patrick Kearns This story was update after publication to include more information about what is known and unknown about Zillow’s Instant Offers program and how Zillow refers leads and selects agent and broker partners.
