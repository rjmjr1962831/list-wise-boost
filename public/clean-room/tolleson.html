History

Tolleson was founded in 1912 by Walter Tolleson, who established a vegetable shipping operation that gave the town its economic foundation.

The city became known as the 'Vegetable Capital of the World' during its agricultural peak, shipping lettuce and produce nationwide.

Tolleson incorporated in 1929 and has maintained independence while Phoenix sprawl surrounded it.

Life in Tolleson

Tolleson maintains working-class West Valley character with strong Hispanic community heritage from agricultural roots. The small city provides municipal services without the scale of surrounding Phoenix while industrial development brings employment. Residents accept limited amenities for affordability and community connection.

Buyer Profile

First-time buyers seeking the lowest entry points to the West Valley dominate demand. Workers at nearby distribution centers appreciate proximity. Investors find rental yields attractive at compressed prices. Multi-generational families find affordable options for extended households.

Market Trends

Tolleson median prices hover around $320,000, representing deep value in the Phoenix metro. Days on market average 35 days. Older housing stock requires updating but prices remain highly competitive.

Local Insider Tip
