Youngtown holds the distinction of being Arizona's first age-restricted community, established in 1954 as a retirement destination before Sun City existed. This tiny municipality of about one square mile sits surrounded by Sun City and Peoria, maintaining its independent identity as an affordable alternative to larger retirement communities. History

Youngtown was founded in 1954 by developer Ben Schleifer, predating Del Webb's Sun City by six years and pioneering the Arizona retirement community concept.

The community was originally called Youngtown to attract active retirees, emphasizing vitality rather than aging.

Youngtown incorporated in 1960, becoming one of Arizona's smallest municipalities by area.

Life in Youngtown

Youngtown maintains small-town retirement community character with lower costs than surrounding Sun City. Residents appreciate the affordable entry point into age-restricted living without the extensive amenities and associated fees of larger communities. The town attracts practical retirees focused on value.

Buyer Profile

Budget-conscious retirees seeking age-restricted community at the lowest cost dominate the market. Some buyers use Youngtown as stepping stone before moving to larger Sun City homes. Snowbirds seeking affordable winter residence find manageable carrying costs.

Market Trends

Youngtown median prices run around $275,000, making it the most affordable age-restricted option in the West Valley. Days on market average 50 days. Smaller older homes require updating but offer exceptional value.
