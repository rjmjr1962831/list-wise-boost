History

Prescott Valley began as a retirement community called Del E. Webb's Sun City Prescott Valley in the 1960s, though Webb withdrew and the community evolved into a more diverse city with families and working-age residents.

The Fain family homesteaded much of the land that became Prescott Valley in the 1870s, ranching cattle and sheep on the high desert grasslands. Fain Park and other landmarks preserve the family name.

Rapid growth transformed Prescott Valley from a population of 8,000 in 1990 to over 50,000 today, making it the largest city in Yavapai County and one of Arizona's fastest-growing communities.

Life in Prescott Valley

Prescott Valley has evolved from Prescott's affordable alternative into a community with its own identity. Families drawn by schools and value shop at the Gateway Mall and attend events at Findlay Toyota Center. Retirees appreciate the lower prices while still accessing Prescott's historic downtown. The vibe is practical and unpretentious, with big-box retail and chain restaurants serving a population focused on value. Community events like the Prescott Valley Days festival create local traditions, while the surrounding national forest provides hiking and outdoor access.

Buyer Profile

Prescott Valley attracts value-focused buyers seeking mountain lifestyle at lower prices. Young families find schools, parks, and affordable homes that don't exist in Prescott proper. Retirees on fixed incomes appreciate the lower costs while remaining close to Prescott's healthcare and amenities. Remote workers seeking mountain living without Flagstaff's prices discover they can afford larger homes here. Investors have purchased rental properties to serve the growing population.

Market Trends

Prescott Valley offers the region's best value, with median home prices around $425,000, roughly $100,000 below Prescott proper. New construction continues to absorb demand, with several subdivisions offering homes from the high $300,000s. Days on market average 50-60 days, with inventory around 3 months of supply. The rental market has tightened as the population has grown, with average rents around $1,450.

Local Insider Tip

The Peavine Trail follows an abandoned railroad grade along the dramatic granite dells for 11 miles, offering flat, easy walking and biking with views of the rock formations that make Watson Lake famous.

Why People Move to Prescott Valley

Affordable Four-Season Living: With median home prices around $425,000, Prescott Valley offers mountain climate and outdoor access at lower cost than Prescott or Flagstaff.

Family-Friendly Amenities: The aquatic center, parks, youth sports facilities, and Findlay Toyota Center events create genuine family infrastructure.

Retail Hub: As the commercial center for the Prescott region, the city offers shopping and services that residents of surrounding communities lack.

Outdoor Access: Prescott National Forest surrounds the city, with hiking, mountain biking, and camping available within minutes.
