History

Nogales grew as a border crossing point after the Gadsden Purchase in 1854 established the current boundary.

The town split into Nogales, Arizona and Nogales, Sonora when the international border was formalized, creating the 'Ambos Nogales' (Both Nogales) community.

Cross-border commerce has driven the economy since the Free Trade Agreement accelerated trade.

Life in Nogales

Nogales embraces its binational identity, with Spanish as common as English and Mexican culture permeating daily life. The economy depends on cross-border trade and immigration. The community includes longtime families with roots on both sides of the border. The atmosphere is distinctly different from Phoenix, with genuine Mexican influence rather than tourist-oriented Southwestern style.
