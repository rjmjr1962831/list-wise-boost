Gilbert Real Estate Market Intelligence

Gilbert exploded from a sleepy farming community of 5,000 in 1980 to one of America's largest towns, a family-focused powerhouse that consistently ranks among the nation's safest cities and best places to raise children. The transformation has been breathtaking: cotton fields and dairy farms gave way to master-planned communities, top-rated schools, and a heritage district that has become the Valley's most charming small-town downtown. Gilbert proves that rapid growth and quality of life aren't mutually exclusive.

History

Gilbert was founded in 1902 as a railroad town on the Arizona Eastern Railway, named after Bobby Gilbert, a railway contractor who built the railroad grade. The town served as a shipping point for the surrounding agricultural region, earning its nickname 'Hay Shipping Capital of the World.'

For most of the 20th century, Gilbert remained an agricultural community producing hay, cotton, and dairy products. As late as 1980, the town's population was just 5,717, with farms still dominating the landscape.

The population explosion began in the 1990s when master-planned communities started converting farmland to subdivisions. Gilbert grew from 29,000 in 1990 to 109,000 in 2000 to over 280,000 today, making it possibly the fastest-growing municipality in American history during that period.

Life in Gilbert

Gilbert has cultivated a distinctive family-focused identity that shapes everything from its events calendar to its restaurant scene. The Heritage District hosts seasonal festivals including the beloved Gilbert Days celebration each November, with a parade, carnival, and community performances. Youth sports dominate weekends, with Gilbert's fields and complexes hosting tournaments that draw teams from across the Southwest. The food scene has matured beyond chain restaurants, with the Heritage District now featuring James Beard-nominated chefs and craft beverage makers. And unlike many fast-growing suburbs, Gilbert has fostered genuine community connections through neighborhood associations, community gardens, and local traditions.

Buyer Profile

Gilbert attracts families with laser focus. The typical buyer is a dual-income household with children, often relocating from California, the Midwest, or elsewhere in the Valley seeking top-rated schools and a safe community. Tech workers from nearby Chandler and Mesa represent a significant segment, as do healthcare professionals from Banner and Mercy Gilbert hospitals. First-time homebuyers often target Gilbert's more affordable southern reaches toward Queen Creek, while move-up buyers seek larger lots in established neighborhoods near the Heritage District.

Market Trends

Gilbert commands premium prices reflecting its schools and safety reputation, with the median home price around $545,000. The market has stabilized from pandemic highs but remains competitive, particularly for homes near top-rated schools. Days on market average around 40-50 days, faster than the Valley average. Inventory is tight, with less than 2 months of supply in desirable neighborhoods. New construction continues in the southeastern quadrant toward Queen Creek, offering entry-level options in the mid-$400,000s, though lot sizes have shrunk and HOA restrictions have tightened.

Local Insider Tip

The Gilbert Rotary Centennial Observatory at the Riparian Preserve offers free public stargazing every Friday and Saturday evening, weather permitting. Volunteers set up telescopes for views of planets, nebulae, and galaxies that rival dedicated dark-sky sites, all within a suburban park setting.

Why People Move to Gilbert

Top-Rated Schools: Gilbert Public Schools and Higley Unified School District consistently rank among Arizona's best, with multiple schools earning National Blue Ribbon recognition and strong college placement rates.

Safest Large City: Gilbert regularly ranks as one of America's safest cities with populations over 100,000, with violent crime rates a fraction of national averages.

Family-First Amenities: Over 90 parks, 127 miles of trails, exceptional youth sports facilities, and family-friendly events create an infrastructure specifically designed for raising children.

Proximity to Employment: Located centrally in the East Valley, Gilbert offers quick commutes to Intel and Chandler's tech corridor, Mesa's aerospace employers, and downtown Phoenix via the 202 freeway.
