History

Avondale began as a farming community in the early 1900s, with the name possibly derived from the abundance of avocados once grown in the area. Agriculture dominated the economy until suburban development accelerated in the 1970s.

Phoenix Raceway (originally Phoenix International Raceway) opened in 1964, bringing motorsports to the West Valley. The track has hosted NASCAR, IndyCar, and other racing series, becoming a defining feature of Avondale's identity.

Luke Air Force Base, located partly in Avondale and partly in Glendale, has shaped the community since World War II, with military families comprising a significant portion of the population.

Life in Avondale

Avondale's culture reflects its diverse, working-class population. Latino families comprise a significant portion of residents, supporting excellent Mexican restaurants and markets. Military families from Luke AFB add another dimension. NASCAR race weekends transform the city, with fans filling hotels and restaurants. Youth sports and school activities provide common ground across communities. The vibe is unpretentious and practical, with residents focused on value and community rather than prestige.

Buyer Profile

First-time buyers seeking affordability dominate Avondale's market, often purchasing their first home here before moving to pricier suburbs. Military families from Luke AFB appreciate proximity to the base and affordable housing. Hispanic families drawn by cultural community and value make up a significant segment. Investors have purchased rental properties to serve the working population. And NASCAR fans occasionally seek homes near the raceway for convenient race weekend access.

Market Trends

Avondale offers the West Valley's best value for established housing, with median home prices around $380,000. The market serves price-sensitive buyers, with days on market around 50-60 days. Older housing stock dominates, with less new construction than in Buckeye or Goodyear. The rental market serves working families, with average rents around $1,500. Appreciation has lagged faster-growing suburbs, but the value proposition attracts steady demand.
