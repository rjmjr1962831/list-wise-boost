Anaheim Real Estate Market Intelligence

Anaheim anchors Orange County as the county's largest city and home to Disneyland, the Happiest Place on Earth that draws 18 million annual visitors. Beyond the resort district, Anaheim offers diverse neighborhoods from historic downtown to suburban tracts, all benefiting from Orange County's strong economy. The city has invested heavily in urban development around Angel Stadium and Honda Center.

History

Anaheim was founded in 1857 by German immigrants who established a wine-producing colony.

Disneyland opened in 1955 on 160 acres of orange groves, transforming Anaheim's identity and economy permanently.

The Anaheim Ducks won the 2007 Stanley Cup, giving the city its first major sports championship.

Life in Anaheim

Anaheim's identity splits between resort district tourist economy and residential neighborhoods where families live normal lives. The Hispanic community represents over half the population. Disneyland employs 30,000 cast members, creating a unique workforce. Downtown revival and Packing District development have created local gathering spots beyond the tourist zones.

Buyer Profile

Families seeking Orange County schools at below-Irvine prices drive demand. Disney and tourism workers need housing near employment. Sports fans value arena and stadium proximity. Some investors target vacation rental potential near Disneyland.

Market Trends

Anaheim median prices approach $850,000, reflecting Orange County premiums. Days on market average 35 days. Anaheim Hills commands premiums over flatland areas. Properties near Disneyland attract investor interest despite HOA restrictions on short-term rentals.

Local Insider Tip

The Anaheim Packing District transformed a 1919 citrus packing house into a food hall and gathering space that locals prefer to tourist-heavy Downtown Disney.

Why People Move to Anaheim

Disneyland: World-famous theme park creates enormous tourist economy.

Sports Venues: Angels and Ducks provide major league entertainment.

Central Location: Easy access to beaches, mountains, and Los Angeles.

Diverse Neighborhoods: Options from urban downtown to suburban family areas.
