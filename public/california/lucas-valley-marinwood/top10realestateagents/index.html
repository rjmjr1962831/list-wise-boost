History

The Lucas Valley subdivision, developed in the 1960s, is famous for its concentration of over 500 Eichler homes, celebrated for their post-and-beam construction, floor-to-ceiling glass walls, and atriums, representing a significant chapter in California modernist architecture.

The area was originally part of the Rancho San Jose land grant and later owned by the Lucas family, for whom the valley is named, prior to its mid-20th century development into a planned residential community.

Marinwood, the eastern portion of the community, was developed concurrently and is known for its strong community identity centered around the Marinwood Community Center and Park, designed by architect John Carl Warnecke.

Life in Lucas Valley-Marinwood

The lifestyle in Lucas Valley-Marinwood is defined by an active, family-oriented, and nature-appreciating community. Residents enjoy a quiet, neighborly atmosphere with a strong focus on outdoor living, from hiking and mountain biking on the extensive trail networks of the surrounding open space preserves to community gatherings at the Marinwood Park. The culture is low-key yet sophisticated, with easy access to the amenities of San Rafael and a short commute to San Francisco. There is a palpable pride in the area's architectural heritage and a collective commitment to preserving its natural setting and tight-knit community feel.
