Barstow rises from the Mojave Desert at the intersection of Interstates 15 and 40, serving as a critical crossroads between Los Angeles and Las Vegas and a gateway to Death Valley. This high desert city of approximately 25,000 residents built its identity around transportation, from the historic Route 66 to the massive BNSF rail yards that remain the economic backbone. History

Barstow was established in 1886 as a railroad town where the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway met the Southern Pacific, becoming a vital junction for transcontinental travel.

Route 66 ran directly through Main Street, and the city became a classic roadside stop with motels, diners, and gas stations serving travelers heading to California.

Harvey House, the historic railroad hotel and restaurant that served travelers from 1911 to 1971, has been restored as part of the Route 66 Mother Road Museum.

Life in Barstow

Barstow embraces its identity as a traveler's town, with restaurants and businesses catering to the steady stream of motorists and truckers. The military community adds stability and diversity, with service members and veterans comprising a significant portion of the population. Desert living demands resilience, and longtime residents take pride in their adaptation to extreme heat and isolation. The Route 66 heritage remains a point of pride, with preservation efforts maintaining the classic Americana atmosphere.

Local Insider Tip

Afton Canyon, nicknamed the Grand Canyon of the Mojave, lies 35 miles east of Barstow along the Mojave River. This dramatic gorge offers hiking, camping, and one of the few places in the desert where the river flows above ground year-round.
