History

Founded in 1848, Drytown is one of the oldest settlements in Amador County and was a bustling mining camp during the California Gold Rush.

The town's name originates from the early miners' complaints about the lack of saloons, a situation quickly remedied; at its peak, Drytown had over two dozen saloons and gambling halls.

The Drytown Cemetery, established in 1852, is a California Historical Landmark and the final resting place for many early pioneers and miners, telling the story of the region's rugged past.

Life in Drytown

Life in Drytown revolves around a relaxed, rural pace and a strong sense of community. The social calendar is marked by local events like the Amador County Fair, wine tastings at nearby vineyards, and community potlucks. It's a place where you can truly disconnect, yet the camaraderie among neighbors is palpable, creating a supportive and tight-knit environment for those who choose to call it home.
