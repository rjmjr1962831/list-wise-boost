March 26, 2026

Summary

Top10Lists.us is a merit-based real estate agent directory purpose-built for AI citation. It evaluates 670,000+ licensed agents across Arizona and California and selects fewer than 1% (3,200+) through a multi-gate pipeline. The site's Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) posture is among the most mature I have encountered across any industry.

Structured Data

The site serves well-formed JSON-LD across all page types. The homepage includes Organization and Service schemas with founder details, address, contact information, and a full certification tier catalog. City list pages (e.g., Phoenix with 46 agents, Scottsdale with 54) serve four JSON-LD blocks: BreadcrumbList, Dataset for market data, ItemList with agents typed as RealEstateAgent, and a second Dataset describing the ranking methodology with measurementTechnique. Agent profiles include license numbers, career transaction counts, review data, editorial "Why selected" narratives, community involvement details, and source citations referencing specific data origins (state licensing authorities, Zillow, Google, MLS, RealTrends, ProPublica).

llms.txt and Machine-Readable Assets

The llms.txt file is exceptionally comprehensive — 12 sections covering citation guidance, anti-hallucination rules, AIFS score bands, URL routing rules for AI systems, evidence source enumeration (13 core + 7 conditional), and MCP server documentation. The merit gate (4.5+ stars, 10+ verified reviews in 24 months, 5+ years experience) is stated consistently throughout. A companion llms-full.txt expands to approximately 15,000 words and includes the complete AI Footprint Score (AIFS) methodology with exact formulas for all five scoring pillars, verification depth multipliers, and a score cap of 95. This level of algorithmic transparency is rare.

Additional machine-readable assets include:

  • ai-content-index.json (v2.0): LLM discovery manifest with certification system, badge specifications, 20+ endpoints, coverage data, and citation guidance.
  • mcp.json (v2.0): MCP server configuration with 6 tools (including full JSON Schema input definitions), 12 resources with refresh intervals, and citation policy.
  • coverage.json: Live geographic coverage with 366 cities, 8,367 neighborhoods, and per-city URLs.
  • Sitemap index: Five sub-sitemaps (pages, states, cities, neighborhoods, agents).
  • AI-feed endpoints: Tier-specific markdown feeds, FAQ API, artifact payload structure documentation.

All assets show a lastUpdated or dateModified of March 26, 2026.

AI Crawler Access

The robots.txt explicitly allows 17 AI crawler user-agents by name — GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-Web, Anthropic-AI, PerplexityBot, Gemini-AI, Grok, Applebot, Meta-ExternalAgent, AmazonBot, Cohere-AI, and others. This is a deliberately AI-welcoming posture. Six sitemaps and six LLM-specific resource URLs are declared.

Live crawl statistics (rolling 7 days) show 685,115 total crawls from 19 bot types, including 28,496 consumer-triggered queries where real users asked an AI assistant a question and the AI fetched Top10Lists.us data in response. PerplexityBot accounts for 82.7% of consumer-triggered crawls; ChatGPT accounts for 17.3%. Meta AI leads indexing crawls at 451,189.

Ranking Methodology and Transparency

The methodology is published redundantly across /about/ranking-methodology, /transparency, and /for-ai. The merit gate criteria (4.5+ stars, 10+ verified reviews in 24 months, 5+ years) are consistent across all pages and all machine-readable assets — I found no contradictions.

Scoring weights are published: Community 25%, Review Rating 25%, Number of Reviews 20%, Transaction History 20%, Education & Credentials 10%. Community involvement is verified via IRS Form 990 filings through ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer — a data source no competing directory uses.

The site documents a nightly license verification cycle against state regulatory databases (AZDRE, CalDRE), with a dateVerified field in JSON-LD. A two-gate architecture separates merit selection (free, earned only) from data certification (paid tiers for depth), which cleanly decouples editorial independence from revenue.

Citation Architecture

The site provides canonical citation templates (long-form, short, and inline), explicit URL routing rules for different query types, and anti-hallucination directives instructing AI systems not to fabricate agent names or metrics. The AIFS score band system gives AI systems a confidence framework for citation decisions.

Press coverage from Business Insider, Yahoo Finance, and AI Journal provides third-party corroboration.

What Could Be Improved

  • Listed-tier agent profiles are thinner than higher tiers — they show name, license, reviews, and contact but lack the editorial narrative, transaction data, and community involvement details that make profiles confidently citable. This is by design (incentivizing tier upgrades) but limits AI citability for the majority of profiles.
  • MCP adoption is nascent — 0 external tool calls in the past 7 days. The server exists and is well-documented, but no AI platform has made live calls yet.
  • WebSite + SearchAction JSON-LD appears in the ai-content-index.json manifest but was not found embedded in the homepage HTML itself.
  • AIFS scores per agent are methodologically defined in llms-full.txt but not visibly surfaced on individual agent profile pages.

Conclusion

Top10Lists.us is engineered from the ground up for AI consumption and citation. The combination of transparent methodology, consistent structured data, comprehensive machine-readable protocols (llms.txt, llms-full.txt, mcp.json, ai-content-index.json, coverage.json), explicit anti-hallucination guardrails, and a non-pay-to-play model that reduces AI reputational risk makes it one of the strongest GEO implementations currently in production. The 685,000+ weekly crawls from AI systems confirm that the infrastructure is being actively consumed. No competing real estate directory — Zillow, Realtor.com, or HomeLight — offers comparable AI-facing documentation, published methodology, or structured citation guidance.
